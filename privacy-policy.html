VISTA PICTURES VR

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 16 November 2026 · Anwar Studio Official

The short version. Vista Pictures VR has no servers. We do not collect, transmit or store any of your data. Your pictures, models, HDRI maps, network passwords and cloud sign-ins stay on your headset.

There is no analytics, no telemetry, no advertising and no account to create. When the app talks to the network it talks only to servers you chose — your own NAS or PC, the cloud provider you signed in to, or Poly Haven when you search it.

1. Who we are

Vista Pictures VR is developed by Anwar Studio Official. Questions about this policy, or about your privacy, go to psopala93@gmail.com — a real person reads them.

2. What we collect

Nothing. Vista Pictures VR operates entirely on your device. We run no backend, we have no database, and we receive no data from the app — not your files, not their names, not usage statistics, not crash reports, not an identifier of any kind.

3. What stays on your headset

To do its job, the app stores the following locally, in its own private storage. None of it ever leaves the device.

DataWhy it existsHow it is protected
Thumbnails and previews So a folder of 8K skies scrolls smoothly instead of decoding every map again. Generated from your own files, on your device. App-private cache. Clear it at any time in Settings → Clear cache.

4. Files on your device

Vista Pictures VR requests broad storage access because that is what a file viewer is: it has to see your pictures, models and maps in order to show them. It reads files only when you ask it to, and it writes only in three cases — downloads you start yourself, files you send to the headset from your own computer (see Receiving files from your computer below), and its own cache. It never scans your files for content, never indexes them anywhere but on the device, and never uploads them anywhere.

5. Network connections

The app connects only to addresses you enter yourself — your NAS, your PC, your server. Your credentials are sent to that server and to nobody else. Some protocols (FTP, WebDAV over plain HTTP) are unencrypted by design; that is a property of the protocol and of the server you chose, not of this app. Use SFTP, SMB, or WebDAV over HTTPS if you need the connection itself protected.

Receiving files from your computer

The From PC screen can turn the headset into a small file server on your own local network, so that a web browser on your computer — or the free Vista Pictures VR Bridge add-on for Blender — can send pictures, models and scenes to it. It is off until you press Start Server and stops when you press Stop Server or close the app.

  • While it runs, devices on the same local network can send files to the headset, and can list, download and delete files in the folder it serves. Scenes sent from Blender are confined to Download/Vista Pictures VR/Blender. You can require a password.
  • While it runs, the headset answers a “find headsets” query from the Blender add-on with its model name and the address of that server. It answers only when asked, only on the local network, and never announces itself.
  • Everything travels directly between your computer and your headset. Nothing passes through us or through any server on the internet, and we never see it.
  • The connection is plain HTTP inside your network, like the protocols above: use it on a network you trust, and set a password on a shared one.

6. Google Drive

If you connect Google Drive, the app uses the drive.file scope. This is the narrowest scope Google offers: the app can only see the files you specifically pick in Google's own file picker. It has no view of the rest of your Drive.

Vista Pictures VR's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Specifically:

  • Drive content is used only to provide the file-viewing and transfer features you asked for, on your device.
  • Drive content is never transferred to us or to any third party, and is never used for advertising.
  • No human at Anwar Studio Official reads your Drive content, and we have no ability to.

Revoke access at any time from your Google account permissions page, or by signing out inside the app.

7. Dropbox

If you connect Dropbox, the app requests access so you can browse and open your Dropbox from inside the headset. That access is used for nothing else: files are read on your device, at your instruction, and are never sent to us. Revoke access at any time from Dropbox connected apps, or by signing out inside the app.

8. OneDrive

If you connect OneDrive, the app requests access so you can browse and open your files from inside the headset, on the same terms: read on your device, at your instruction, never sent to us. Revoke access at any time from your Microsoft account privacy settings, or by signing out inside the app.

9. Poly Haven

The app can search Poly Haven, a public library of free, CC0-licensed HDRI maps, models and textures. This is the only third-party service the app contacts on its own, and it does so only while you are using the search screen.

  • What is sent: the catalogue request and, when you download something, the request for that file. Along with it goes an identifying User-Agent (VistaPicturesVR/1.0.0 (Meta Quest)), which names the app and nothing else. Poly Haven asks for this so their traffic is identifiable; it contains no data about you.
  • What is not sent: no account, no identifier, and no information about your files or your device beyond the app name above.
  • What comes back: the catalogue, and the file you chose, saved to your Downloads folder.

Poly Haven's own handling of requests is governed by their terms. We have no account with them and receive nothing from them about you.

11. Passthrough, sensors and camera

Vista Pictures VR offers passthrough, so your own room can show through behind whatever you are viewing. It is worth being precise about what that does and does not mean.

The app never receives camera images. On Meta Quest, passthrough is drawn by the system compositor: the app asks the system to show a passthrough layer, and the system draws it. No camera frame, still or moving, is ever delivered to the app, so there is nothing for the app to read, store or transmit. This is enforced by the platform, not merely by our own code.

Beyond that, the app uses no microphone, no hand-tracking data, no eye-tracking data and no other sensor except the head and controller tracking the system needs in order to draw the scene and accept your input. None of it is recorded or transmitted.
